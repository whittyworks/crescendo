A Deeper Approach

Why Cognitive Functions?

Traditional personality tests ask about preferences—introvert or extravert, thinking or feeling. But these questions are easy to "game" if you know which answers lead where.

The Problem with Letter-Based Tests

Most 16-type personality tests ask questions like "Do you prefer being alone or with people?" If you've taken a few tests, you learn to recognize which answers produce which results—making it easy to unconsciously (or consciously) steer toward your desired type.

Cognitive functions go deeper. Instead of asking what you prefer, they reveal how you actually process information. These patterns are harder to fake because they describe the mechanics of your mind, not just your self-perception.

Stress reveals truth. This test also measures how you behave under stress—when your "grip" functions activate and your usual patterns shift. Since we can't easily control our stress responses, these questions provide another layer of accuracy that traditional tests miss entirely.

This test measures all eight functions independently, then matches your profile to the type whose "function stack" best fits your natural patterns.
